Team 2

Finite scheduling · Plan to Inventory · 60 minutes · fictional case

Context

Northvale Gear Works is a fictional mid-sized manufacturer of precision gearboxes supplying agricultural and construction OEMs. Its single plant runs a machining area (12 CNC work centers), heat treatment (one external-partner-style internal furnace cell that batches loads), and assembly. Planning runs in the company ERP: MRP generates planned orders weekly, and schedulers release production orders to the shop floor. Two years ago the plant moved from make-to-stock on all SKUs to a hybrid model with 40% make-to-order variants. Order volume has grown 18% year-over-year, and the product mix has shifted toward smaller lots of high-variant gearsets. The master production schedule is loaded using infinite capacity assumptions, and schedulers manually re-sequence work on spreadsheets each Monday. Finished-goods inventory has grown while on-time availability to the assembly line and to stock replenishment has worsened. Plant leadership has asked a cross-functional team to diagnose the planning problem and propose how the ERP's scheduling capabilities should be used differently before approving any capital spend on new machines.

Challenge

Diagnose why inventory is rising while availability falls, and recommend an ERP-enabled finite scheduling approach for the machining area that improves schedule reliability within the Plan to Inventory value stream — without assuming new machine purchases.

Symptoms

  • Finished-goods and WIP inventory value up 22% in nine months, yet assembly reports part shortages on 1 in 5 build starts.
  • Schedulers override roughly 60% of MRP-proposed start dates each week using a Monday spreadsheet re-sequencing ritual.
  • Average production order lead time in machining has drifted from 9 days to 16 days, while routing standard lead time is still set at 8 days.
  • Heat treatment furnace loads are released in inconsistent batch sizes, causing alternating idle time and 3-day queues ahead of the furnace.
  • Expedite ('hot') tags now cover 35% of open machining orders, and expedited jobs displace scheduled work without system visibility.

Evidence

  • ERP capacity evaluation report (last 8 weeks): Three CNC work centers (gear hobbing cells GH-02, GH-03, GH-05) show planned load at 125–140% of available capacity in weeks 3–8, while four milling centers average 55% load. The MPS was still accepted without change because planning runs with infinite capacity flags on.
  • Order aging extract from production order history: Orders routed through hobbing wait a median of 4.1 days in queue before first operation; queue time in the routing master is set to 0.5 days. 78% of late orders to assembly passed through GH-02 or GH-03.
  • Scheduler interview notes: Schedulers state they re-sequence by customer escalation emails, not by due date or load: 'The ERP dates are fiction, so we trust the spreadsheet. We release extra stock orders early to keep machines busy when a hot job finishes faster than expected.'
  • Inventory analytics dashboard: Safety stock parameters were raised twice in six months on 140 SKUs 'to protect assembly,' but shortage incidents did not fall; 63% of excess finished-goods value sits in low-variant SKUs that rarely short, while high-variant gearsets short repeatedly.
  • Heat treatment cell log: Furnace cycles run whenever a scheduler manually flags 'enough parts'; load quantities ranged from 30% to 110% of optimal batch size across the last 40 cycles, with no ERP batching rule or campaign logic applied.

Your deliverable

A 10-minute team presentation stating the root-cause diagnosis, a phased ERP-enabled finite scheduling recommendation for machining and heat treatment, three measurable success targets, and the top risks with mitigations.

Agenda

  • 5 min – Facilitator introduces the case, ground rules, and deliverable expectations.
  • 15 min – Teams read the brief and evidence; map symptoms to candidate root causes.
  • 15 min – Teams design an ERP-enabled finite scheduling response and phased rollout.
  • 10 min – Teams define success measures, risks, and prepare the presentation.
  • 10 min – Team presentations (10 minutes total) with facilitator challenge questions.
  • 5 min – Facilitator debrief: reveal model diagnosis, compare approaches, capture lessons.
