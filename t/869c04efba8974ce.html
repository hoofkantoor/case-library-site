Team 1

MES · Plan to Inventory · 60 minutes · fictional case

Context

Nordvale Components is a fictional mid-sized manufacturer of precision-machined housings for industrial pumps, operating one plant with three machining cells and an assembly line. Production planning runs in the corporate ERP (MRP nightly), while shop-floor execution is managed in a Manufacturing Execution System (MES) that was introduced two years ago. Operators report production, scrap, and material consumption in the MES; an interface posts these transactions to the ERP. Over the past two quarters, planners have lost confidence in system inventory figures: MRP keeps proposing urgent purchase orders for raw bar stock that physically exists, while finished-goods availability shown to customer service is often higher than what the warehouse can actually ship. Expediting costs and manual stock checks have risen sharply. Plant leadership has asked a cross-functional team to diagnose the root cause and propose an ERP/MES-enabled fix within the current fiscal year, without a full system replacement.

Challenge

Diagnose why planned, reported, and physical inventory have diverged across the ERP and MES, and recommend a practical, phased ERP/MES-enabled solution that restores inventory record accuracy and planning trust within two quarters.

Symptoms

  • MRP generates expedite purchase requisitions for raw bar stock that cycle counters later confirm is physically on hand in the cell-side supermarket.
  • Customer service promises finished goods based on ERP availability, but the warehouse reports shortages at picking roughly 2-3 times per week.
  • Work-in-process value in the ERP has grown 38% year over year while physical WIP on the floor appears visually unchanged.
  • Month-end reconciliation between MES production reports and ERP goods-receipt postings takes finance 4 days and ends with manual adjustment journals.
  • Operators keep paper 'shadow logs' in two of three machining cells because they say the MES terminals 'lose' their confirmations during shift change.

Evidence

  • ERP-MES interface error log (last 90 days): 1,842 production confirmation messages failed posting to the ERP with error 'order status locked' and were queued; 61% of failures occur between 21:30 and 23:00, overlapping the nightly MRP run and order-settlement batch job. Failed messages are retried only once, then parked in a manual reprocessing queue that is cleared on average every 9 days.
  • Cycle count program summary, Q3: Raw material locations show 94% record accuracy, but backflush-relevant components at cell-side supermarkets show only 71% accuracy. Variances are predominantly negative in the ERP (system shows less than physical) for bar stock and positive for finished goods (system shows more than physically shippable).
  • MES configuration review memo: Material consumption is backflushed at final operation confirmation using the standard bill of materials. Scrap recorded at intermediate operations in the MES is not interfaced; the integration spec marks intermediate scrap as 'phase 2 - not implemented'. Average recorded intermediate scrap is 3.1% of starts.
  • Warehouse supervisor interview notes: Finished-goods receipts are posted in the ERP when the MES final confirmation interfaces, but physical putaway can lag by up to a shift; quality holds placed in the MES do not create a blocked-stock status in the ERP, so held units appear available to customer service.
  • Planning KPI dashboard extract: Expedite POs for bar stock up 54% versus prior year; planner manual overrides of MRP proposals up from 8% to 31% of order proposals; average of 11 'emergency' physical stock checks per week requested by planners, consuming an estimated 14 warehouse labor hours weekly.

Your deliverable

A 10-minute team presentation covering: (1) root-cause diagnosis linked explicitly to the evidence, (2) a phased ERP/MES remediation plan achievable within two quarters, and (3) measurable success criteria with baseline and target values.

Agenda

  • 5 min – Facilitator introduces the case, objectives, deliverable, and ground rules
  • 10 min – Individual reading of the brief and evidence pack; silent note-taking
  • 15 min – Team diagnosis: map symptoms to evidence, agree on root cause(s)
  • 12 min – Solution design: phased ERP/MES remediation plan with success measures
  • 10 min – Team presentations (10 minutes total, timeboxed per team)
  • 8 min – Facilitator challenge questions, model-response debrief, and wrap-up
